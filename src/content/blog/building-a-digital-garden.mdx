## Why a digital garden?

A traditional blog is an archive ordered by time. A digital garden gives ideas room to keep growing. The goal here is not publishing frequency, but a **low-friction writing system**: adding one Markdown file is enough to validate, build, and publish a new essay.

## Separating content from presentation

Astro Content Collections provide a structured contract for frontmatter:

```ts
const schema = z.object({
  title: z.string(),
  pubDate: z.coerce.date(),
  tags: z.array(z.string()),
});
```

The content remains portable while the interface can evolve independently. Even if the rendering framework changes one day, every essay remains plain text.

### Progressive enhancement

Search and theme switching require a little JavaScript; everything else ships as static HTML by default. This keeps the site readable on slow networks and even when scripts fail.

## Performance as a design constraint

Motion should clarify hierarchy rather than compete for attention. This site follows three rules:

1. Prefer `transform` and `opacity`;
2. Cap the Canvas pixel ratio and particle count;
3. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

> Restraint does not mean less expression. It means giving every expression a reason.

## What comes next

A digital garden is never truly “finished.” Next, I plan to strengthen the links between essays and add more small, focused frontend experiments.
